### 3. Normalized expression

Raw counts are difficult to compare when spots have different total counts. I applied the formula already specified in the engine design:

\[
x=\ln\left(1+10{,}000\times\frac{\text{gene count}}{\text{spot library size}}\right)
\]

It has three steps:

1. Divide the gene’s count by the spot’s total counts.
2. Multiply by 10,000 to put the proportions on a common scale.
3. Apply `ln(1 + value)` to compress large values while keeping measured zeros at zero.

For example, a count of 1 in a library of 4 becomes:

\[
\ln(1+10{,}000\times1/4)=\ln(2501)\approx7.8244
\]

Counts of 2 in a library of 8 give the same normalized value because the proportion is identical.

This adjusts for total library size. It does **not** remove differences in cell composition or all technical effects.

### 4. Calculated within-mouse spatial comparisons

For every feature in every mouse, I separated retained spots into:

- **Annotated spots**
- **Other spots**

Then I calculated:

| Measurement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Group size | Number of retained spots in that group |
| Mean expression | Average normalized expression, including measured zeros |
| Detection fraction | Fraction of spots with a raw count greater than zero |
| Difference | Annotated mean minus other mean |

For instance, a detection fraction of `0.25` means that the feature had at least one measured count in 25% of the group’s retained spots.

A positive difference means higher average expression in the annotated region. A negative difference means higher expression in the other region.

The difference is measured in **log-normalized expression units**. It is not a log fold change, a significance test, or evidence that changing the gene would improve recovery.

This produced **193,710 rows**: 32,285 features × six mice.
