## My request:
Why does it use the smaller mouse contrast

**Codex:** It uses the smaller contrast to reward **enrichment reproduced in both mice**. A large effect in one mouse cannot compensate for a weak or opposite effect in the other.

For your example:

- Mouse 1: **+1.2** — higher expression in the annotated region.
- Mouse 2: **−0.1** — slightly lower expression there.

Their average is +0.55, which hides the disagreement. Taking the smaller contrast and truncating it to zero means this time point earns no reproducible-enrichment score.

This is a deliberately conservative project rule, not a statistical significance test. With only two mice per time point, it can also penalize a real effect if one mouse’s measurement is noisy.
