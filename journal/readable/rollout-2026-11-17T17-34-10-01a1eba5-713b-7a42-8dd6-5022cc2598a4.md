**Codex:** ## What data do you have?

“I have **10x Visium spatial transcriptomics data from six mouse livers** after acetaminophen-induced injury. There are **three time points: 24, 48, and 72 hours**, with two different mice at each time point. The expression matrix has **32,285 gene features by 17,370 tissue spots**. The measurements represent local RNA abundance, and a zero means no RNA for that gene was detected at that spot.” [Dataset and quality checks](/Users/praneelhurry/Documents/GitHub/02-120project/SPATIAL_EVIDENCE.md)

## What method do you use?

“I use **library-size normalization, within-mouse regional expression contrasts, and a weighted target-prioritization score**.”

1. **Normalize each spot.** I divide each gene’s count by the spot’s total UMI count across all features, multiply by 10,000, and apply `ln(1 + value)`. This accounts for differences in how much RNA was captured across spots.
2. **Compare regions within each mouse.** For each gene, I subtract the mean normalized expression in other spots from the mean in annotated spots. At 48 and 72 hours, the labels identify collagen-enriched regions. At 24 hours, the labels identify manually outlined necrotic regions, which I show separately. The comparison is between regions on the same injured liver section. [Source study methods](https://weizmann.elsevierpure.com/ws/portalfiles/portal/111312424/is_Stem-Cell-Stem_The%20spatiotemporal%20program_AM_%2022.pdf)
3. **Reward agreement between mice.** At each of 48 and 72 hours, I take the smaller of the two mouse differences. For example, if one mouse has a difference of `+0.8` and the other has `−0.2`, that time point contributes zero. I set negative values to zero, use `x / (1 + x)` to put positive values on a bounded scale, and average the two time-point scores.
4. **Rank human target hypotheses.** I require a high-confidence one-to-one mouse-to-human gene match, positive regional contrasts in both mice at one or both ranking time points, and complete scoring evidence. The final score weights **spatial evidence at 60%**, **small-molecule tractability at 25%**, and **hepatic fibrosis association at 15%**. Tractability uses the highest applicable Open Targets evidence tier for each protein. The ranking helps decide what to investigate next. It cannot establish whether blocking or activating a target would help. [Scoring rubric](/Users/praneelhurry/Documents/GitHub/02-120project/ENGINE_DESIGN.md)
