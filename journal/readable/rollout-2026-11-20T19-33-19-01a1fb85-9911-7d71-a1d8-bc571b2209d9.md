**Codex:** The galectin-3 model estimates **how much of a compound is needed to reduce a binding signal by half** in one laboratory assay. It takes a compound’s chemical structure, turns it into a fingerprint that records structural features, and feeds that fingerprint into a Random Forest trained on 226 compounds.

### How to read a prediction

The app reports **pIC50**, a logarithmic form of IC50. A one-unit increase in pIC50 means a tenfold decrease in the concentration needed to reach the same assay endpoint. Higher pIC50 therefore means stronger activity in this assay.

For example, **CHEMBL5761102** had a measured pIC50 of **6.24**, equivalent to about **571 nM**. The model predicted **6.57**, equivalent to about **267 nM**. It overestimated the compound’s activity, predicting that roughly half the measured concentration would be needed. The row also carries a similarity flag because even its closest training compound fell below the similarity range observed in validation. That flag describes chemical resemblance. It gives no numerical estimate of prediction error. [Prediction table and plot](https://spatial-target-evidence.vercel.app/?view=predictions)

### How well it worked

The frozen model was tested on **48 compounds across 29 chemical scaffolds** that were absent from training and validation. Its RMSE was **0.482 pIC50**, compared with **0.697** for a baseline that predicted the training-set average for every compound. The model improved on that baseline in this assay, with nine test compounds flagged for low similarity to the training set. [Saved evaluation](/Users/praneelhurry/Documents/GitHub/02-120project/results/compound_activity/heldout_evaluation.json)

The held-out compounds already had experimental measurements, which let us check the predictions. For a genuinely untested compound, the prediction is a hypothesis about this assay. Whether the compound changes galectin-3 signaling in liver tissue, improves recovery, or is safe requires separate evidence.
