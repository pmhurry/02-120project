### 1. Created a plotting script

It loads the expression matrix and metadata through our existing loader, which matches spots by their full barcode. That ensures each expression measurement is connected to the correct mouse, annotation, and coordinates.

The script generates two SVG figures and a CSV table. SVG files open in a browser and stay sharp when enlarged. It uses Python’s standard library, so no additional packages are needed.

### 2. Plotted annotations and expression for one mouse

The [spatial figure](results/spatial.svg) contains two maps of the same mouse, `t_24h_m1`, collected at 24 hours.

**The left map shows the authors’ annotations:**

- Blue: annotated fibrogenic spot.
- Light gray: other spot.

This map includes all **3,075 metadata spots** for that mouse because their coordinates and annotations are available even when expression is missing.

**The right map shows normalized `Col1a1` expression:**

- Gray: expression was not measured in our subset, or could not be normalized.
- Pale yellow: measured expression of zero.
- Darker brown: higher normalized expression.

Only **62 spots** in this mouse have expression measurements in the supplied subset. That makes the expression map sparse, even though the annotation map is complete.

The distinction between gray and pale yellow matters: **missing expression tells us nothing about whether the gene was active; measured zero means no counts for that gene were detected in that spot.**

Both maps preserve the tissue’s coordinate proportions. The vertical coordinate increases downward, matching image coordinates. This is a map of spot positions; I have not added a tissue-image background.

### 3. Reused the normalization we defined yesterday

For each measured spot, the script calculates:

```text
normalized expression = ln(1 + gene count / total spot counts × 10,000)
```

The total spot count includes all gene rows, rather than only the selected gene.

For example, two spots with:

```text
Spot A: 10 Col1a1 counts / 1,000 total counts
Spot B: 20 Col1a1 counts / 2,000 total counts
```

both become:

```text
ln(1 + 100) ≈ 4.615
```

Their raw counts differ, but their relative expression is the same.

**The figures show an individual gene’s normalized expression, not yesterday’s three-gene collagen score.** The script reuses the checked normalization code, but does not plot its centered score.

### 4. Compared annotated groups within each mouse

The [group-comparison figure](results/group_comparison.svg) shows two groups for each of the six mice:

- Annotated fibrogenic spots.
- Other spots.

For each group, I averaged its measured spots’ normalized expression values:

```text
group mean = sum of normalized spot values / number of scorable spots
```

That means we normalize and log-transform each spot first, then average. We do not average raw counts first.

Each mouse remains separate, so the plot has **12 rows: six mice × two groups**. This lets us see whether the two mice at a time point agree.

The labels report three counts:

```text
scorable spots / measured spots / metadata spots
```

Those distinguish how much tissue was annotated from how much expression actually supports the average. All measured spots are scorable in this dataset, but the script also handles a zero-total-count spot as missing.

There are **two mice per time point**. Hundreds of spots from one mouse do not give us hundreds of independent biological replicates.

### 5. Recorded what the comparison shows

For `Col1a1`, the mean normalized values are:

| Time | Mouse | Fibrogenic | Other |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 24 h | m1 | 0.121 | 0.186 |
| 24 h | m2 | 0.165 | 0.043 |
| 48 h | m4 | 1.941 | 0.376 |
| 48 h | m5 | 1.240 | 0.285 |
| 72 h | m1 | 1.448 | 0.303 |
| 72 h | m2 | 1.875 | 0.283 |

At **48 and 72 hours**, both mice show higher `Col1a1` expression in annotated fibrogenic spots. At **24 hours**, the direction differs between the two mice.

These values are averages on the log-normalized scale. They are **not fold changes, protein measurements, or measurements of scar tissue**.

We also need to keep three limitations attached to the result:

- Only 348 of 17,370 spots have expression in this workflow. Some later fibrogenic groups contain just **4–12 measured spots**, so full-data results could differ.
- Collagen expression helped create the 48/72-hour annotations. Agreement with `Col1a1` is therefore a consistency check, not independent validation.
- The 24-hour annotation describes necrotic regions, giving it a different biological meaning from the later collagen-based labels.

I did not run a significance test or claim that the differences establish a general biological effect.
