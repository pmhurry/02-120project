Explain how it works without jargon

---

ASSISTANT:
The app helps you explore one question: **which genes are worth studying further after liver injury?**

Think of it as a list of research leads. Each gene gets a score based on three things:

- **Its pattern in the injured tissue:** Is it more active in the marked region, and do both mice show that pattern?
- **Whether drugs might be able to act on it:** Is there existing evidence that it can be reached or affected by a drug?
- **Its connection to the selected disease:** What evidence links it to liver fibrosis?

The tissue pattern contributes 60% of the score, drug-related evidence 25%, and disease evidence 15%. A gene needs enough information—and a clear mouse-to-human match—to enter the complete ranking. You can still find genes that were left out and see why.

When you select a gene, the app shows the evidence behind its position. You can choose a mouse and see a map of where that gene is active. Changing the mouse or time changes the map, while the main ranking continues to use the combined 48- and 72-hour results.

The app also shows where the human version of that gene is active in normal tissues, along with reported concerns and known drugs. These help you decide what to investigate. **A high rank does not prove that changing the gene would help, and no reported concerns does not mean it is safe.**

The app displays results we already calculated and checked; clicking around does not change the scoring rules.
